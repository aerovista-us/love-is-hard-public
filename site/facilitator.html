Facilitator Center

Teach pattern literacy without turning the framework into a verdict about a person.

Session Rhythm

  1. Define the distinction.
  2. Contrast healthy and distorted versions.
  3. Rewrite one sentence.
  4. Practice a pause or repair script.
  5. Name what remains uncertain.

Facilitator Limits

Do not diagnose, adjudicate private motives, solicit evidence dumps, or invite participants to weaponize the terms in the same week they learn them.

Safety Lane

Fear, coercion, threats, stalking, physical danger, and retaliation require direct safety planning or qualified support. Communication tools do not replace safety action.

Session Plans

Use each block as a compact facilitation guide. Keep the work focused on pattern literacy, not adjudicating private motives.

1Name the PiecesFive-Part Separator
Session objective
Start with what you know: what you are feeling. Then separate the meaning you made from what has actually been observed or established.
Opening question
Where does this pattern show up before anyone has to decide who is right?
Healthy example
I feel scared and need clarity.
Distorted example
You obviously do not care.
Practice exercise
Rewrite one loaded sentence into five parts: what I felt, what I interpreted, what I claimed, what I needed, and what boundary or capacity limit was present.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if participants use the distinction to invalidate a feeling or force agreement with a claim.
Closing script
A feeling gets care. A claim gets checked. A request gets made clearly.
2Hear ClearlyShared Definition Check

Healthy example

Distorted example
If you hear me, you must admit my version is true.
Practice exercise
Choose one charged word such as support, care, space, proof, or partner. Write both definitions before debating the conflict.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if listening is being treated as mandatory agreement.
Closing script
I can hear impact without surrendering reality.
3Hidden PremiseHidden-Premise Rewrite
Session objective
A need can be valid while the demanded strategy is not. Hidden premises turn needs into accusations.
Opening question
Where does this pattern show up before anyone has to decide who is right?
Healthy example
I need predictable connection.
Distorted example
You must be available all day or I am not chosen.
Practice exercise
Take one demand and split it into need, strategy, hidden claim, and clean request.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if a need becomes one non-negotiable strategy or a character accusation.
Closing script
The need may be valid even when the demanded strategy needs revision.
4Proof PressureProof-to-Request Script
Session objective

Healthy example

Distorted example
Say it again differently until I feel certain.
Practice exercise
Turn a proof demand into a time-bound request: one reassurance sentence, one return time, one self-regulation step.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if reassurance becomes repeated proof, confession, or immediate access pressure.
Closing script
Care can be offered without entering an endless proof loop.
5Escalation MapEscalation Sequence Map
Session objective

Healthy example

Distorted example

Practice exercise
Map a composite conflict in seven stages: distress, assignment, pressure, boundary, escalation, crash, repair-seeking.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if the group judges only the visible reaction and refuses to map the sequence before it.
Closing script
The reaction matters, and the full sequence matters too.
6BoundariesBoundary With Return
Session objective
A boundary can hurt without being abandonment. Repair can include ownership without self-erasure.
Opening question
Where does this pattern show up before anyone has to decide who is right?

Distorted example

Practice exercise
Build a boundary with return: limit, reason, return time, return conditions, and repair topic.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if a boundary is framed as abandonment or repair is used to erase a limit.
Closing script

8AgreementOperating Agreement
Session objective
Convert recurring conflict into shared rules, review dates, and scripts before the next escalation.
Opening question
Where does this pattern show up before anyone has to decide who is right?
Healthy example
We use the agreement definitions first.
Distorted example
Every fight renegotiates the dictionary.
Practice exercise
Choose one recurring conflict. Write the need, allowed strategy, not-allowed behavior, pause script, repair gate, and review date.
Debrief question
What became clearer when the pieces were separated?
Common misuse
Using the lesson language to win the conflict instead of slowing the pattern.
Stop condition
Stop if the agreement becomes scorekeeping, surveillance, or a frozen contract.
Closing script
The agreement is a repair tool, not a weapon.
End with a practical script, not a verdict.
