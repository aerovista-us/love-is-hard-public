Understand the Conflict.
Protect the Conversation.

A practical guide to the difference between needing to be right, needing to be understood, and feeling pressured to agree. Neither person's interpretation becomes a verdict simply because it is strongly felt.

Consent and safety are not negotiable. If one person continues intense conversation after repeated requests to stop, withdraws permission to end, threatens consequences, or makes speaking unsafe, do not start a joint exercise. Prioritize distance, support, and safety as appropriate. The guide does not require anyone to resume, reconcile, or negotiate their right to stop.

Two experiences that can look similar

One perspective: “When I disagree or express how I feel, he keeps correcting me, becomes frustrated, and seems determined to prove me wrong.”

Another perspective: “I understand why the corrections could feel that way. But I need a chance to clarify when I believe my words or intentions have been misrepresented, without being required to accept that interpretation as true.”

Both are accounts worth examining, not independently verified findings. Escalating anger, intimidating conduct, and repeated pressure after a request to stop are separately observable concerns. Those must not be excused by either interpretation.

Clarification is not compelled agreement. “I hear that you felt dismissed” can be sincere without saying “I intended to dismiss you.” Conversely, “That's not what I meant” does not erase the impact of what someone actually said or did.
