Our approach · Fairness and bias

How We Work to Keep Love Is Hard Fair

Love Is Hard Discovery is designed to help people understand communication problems without automatically blaming either person. We have taken deliberate steps to make the work balanced and evidence-based—but no project is automatically free from bias.

1. What we have done to keep it fair

Consider both perspectives

Two people can experience the same conversation differently.

Separate feelings from facts

Feelings deserve acknowledgment; accusations still need evidence.

Allow clarification

People deserve opportunities to explain their meaning without forced agreement.

Check the evidence

Examine what happened, what was intended, and what supports or challenges an explanation.

Challenge confirmation bias

Look for information that may go against our first interpretation.

Include opposing examples

Show cases where an interpretation is correct, incorrect, or incomplete.

Focus on observable behavior

Address interruptions, aggression, pressure, and boundary violations without guessing motives.

Respect boundaries

Either person must be free to stop a conversation without punishment or continued pressure.

2. What we have learned

  • Feeling hurt does not automatically mean someone intended harm.
  • Explaining yourself does not automatically mean avoiding accountability.
  • Being correct does not prove that the evidence was evaluated fairly.
  • Being misunderstood does not excuse aggressive behavior.
  • Wanting acknowledgment does not justify forcing someone to agree.
  • Fairness does not require assuming equal responsibility.

These principles apply to everyone involved.

3. Three questions we use to check for bias

  1. What actually happened?

    Identify exact words or observable behavior.

  2. What did each person say they meant?

    Consider the explanations without automatically accepting or rejecting them.

  3. What evidence supports or challenges those explanations?

    Examine full context, including evidence that might change the initial interpretation.

4. Where we can still improve

  • Original perspective: The project grew from personal experiences, so some examples may unintentionally favor one person's interpretation.
  • Evidence review: Not every recorded conversation has received an independent review of competing explanations.
  • Testing our theories: We should look for cases where our models do not fit, not just cases that support them.
  • Independent feedback: Review from people outside the project could reveal assumptions we have missed.

These are reasons to keep testing and improving the work—not reasons to stop asking questions.

5. Our commitment going forward

  1. Listen to both perspectives.
  2. Separate observable facts from interpretations.
  3. Look for supporting and challenging evidence.
  4. Hold people accountable for actual behavior, not assumed motives.
  5. Revise conclusions when new evidence warrants it.
Fairness does not mean assuming both people are equally right or equally wrong. It means giving both people the same opportunity to be heard, applying the same standards of evidence, and letting the facts guide conclusions.

Why this matters

Love Is Hard is not intended to decide who is the victim, who is the villain, or who wins an argument. It is intended to help people communicate more accurately, recognize harmful patterns, respect boundaries, and take responsibility for their actions.

Our fairness work is meaningful, but it has not been independently validated as bias-free.
