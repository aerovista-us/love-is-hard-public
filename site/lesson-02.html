Session 2: Hear Clearly

Hear Clearly

Hear Without Surrendering Reality

Hearing someone well does not require agreeing with every interpretation or accepting every premise.

10-15 minutesOutput: Shared Definition CheckAudio lens: After We Learned to Hear Each Other
Hearing vs Agreement

Listening can validate pain while still checking the story attached to it.

Healthy version
I hear that this hurt. I do not agree with the motive assigned to me.
Distorted version
If you hear me, you must admit my version is true.
Try this instead
I can hear the impact and still need to clarify the claim.
Watch for
Watch for “so you admit...” after validation.
Meaning Substitution

Exact words and inferred meaning are not the same object.

Healthy version
Let us return to the exact sentence.
Distorted version
What you really meant was the worst possible version.
Try this instead
The sentence I said was ___. The meaning you heard was ___. Let us separate them.
Watch for
Watch for “so what you are saying is...” when it changes the words.
False Agreement

People can use the same word while meaning different things.

Healthy version
Let us define support before we decide whether it happened.
Distorted version
You said support, so you owe the version in my head.
Try this instead
When I say ___, I mean ___. What do you mean?
Watch for
Watch for slippery words that carry different rules.
Practice: Choose one charged word such as support, care, space, proof, or partner. Write both definitions before debating the conflict.
